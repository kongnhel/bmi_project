    Health literacy

    BMI, in context

    A simple number can be useful. It becomes more useful when you understand what it does — and does not — measure.

    The formula BMI

    weight (kg) ÷ height2 (m)

    Weight in kilograms divided by height in metres squared.

    Worked example

    70 kg ÷ (1.75 m × 1.75 m) = 22.9, which falls in the healthy adult range.

    What BMI can tell you

    • A quick, rough comparison of your weight relative to your height.
    • Whether your result sits inside the adult reference range.
    • A starting point for a conversation with a healthcare professional.

    What BMI cannot tell you

    • Your body fat percentage.
    • Whether your weight comes from muscle, fat, bone, or water.
    • Where fat is stored on your body.
    • Your overall health, fitness, or risk of any condition.

    Use it well

    • Adult categories apply to people age 20 and older. Children and teenagers need age- and sex-specific growth charts.
    • Athletes and very muscular people may have a high BMI despite low body fat.
    • Pregnancy, older age, and some health conditions change how BMI should be interpreted.
    • Health risks can differ across populations at the same BMI. Ask a professional about your individual context.

    When to ask for support

    If your BMI is outside the healthy range, has changed unexpectedly, or you have concerns about your weight, eating, or health, talk with a qualified doctor, dietitian, or other healthcare professional. BMI alone should not be used to make medical decisions.
